# The Next Question

**Sixteen ways careful research still goes wrong — and a method for catching them before the report goes out.**

Hygiene checks catch sloppiness. These catch the errors that survive competent work: the report that is confidently wrong, or entirely correct and completely inert.

Free to take, adapt and use. No sign-up, nothing to join.

---

## What's in here

[`SKILL.md`](SKILL.md) is the whole thing. It has three parts:

**1. Analysis** — seven sequential lenses for working through a transcript set, each asking a different question, run in order so later ones build on what earlier ones established.

**2. The report** — a six-act arc so findings read as an explanation rather than a list of complaints, plus the evidence conventions that keep the narrative honest.

**3. Review** — sixteen named failure modes, in five families, to run over a draft.

Most people want part 3. Start there.

---

## The sixteen

**I · Wrong about who you learned from** — invalidates everything downstream, and nothing in the analysis can reveal it.

1. **The Survivor Sample** — were participants selected by the very process being studied?
2. **The Articulate Majority** — would someone who cannot explain their own behaviour have survived the screener?
3. **The Wrong Unit** — a property of the person, or of the system they sit inside?

**II · Wrong about what you measured** — the instrument leaves a fingerprint that gets reported as data.

4. **The Method's Shadow** — does the finding have the same shape as the method?
5. **The Rehearsed Answer** — what they do, or what they like to think they do?
6. **The Prototype Effect** — the design, or novelty plus being watched?

**III · Wrong about what it means** — the data was fine; synthesis was not.

7. **The Smooth Report** — which contradictions survived compression, and which did not?
8. **The Borrowed Frame** — whose language are the themes in?
9. **The Vanishing Denominator** — how many people is "users"?

**IV · True but unfalsifiable** — nothing here is wrong; the problem is that nothing could have been.

10. **The Missing Decision Rule** — before fieldwork, could you write "if X we do A, if Y we do B"?
11. **The Unfalsifiable Finding** — what evidence would have produced the opposite conclusion?
12. **The Quarter-Old Truth** — about people, or about people using this build?

**V · True but inert** — correct, well-evidenced, changes nothing. The most common way research fails.

13. **The Foregone Conclusion** — would this recommendation have appeared if the findings were the opposite?
14. **The Unowned Finding** — name the person whose decision changes, not the team.
15. **The Absent Cost** — what happens if nobody acts?
16. **The Researcher-Dependent Report** — does it work when you are on holiday?

## How to use it

**As a Claude skill.** Save `SKILL.md` into your skills directory and ask Claude to review a draft report. It will run the modes and return an exposure profile.

**As a prompt, in any assistant.** Paste the contents of `SKILL.md` as a system prompt or at the top of a conversation, then give it your draft.

**On paper.** The sixteen questions work perfectly well read down a list with a draft in front of you. The AI version does the work rather than reminding you to.

---

## Two things that decide whether a flag means anything

**Say what the artefact is first.** Working notes, a draft and a final report fail these differently, and family V will fire on any set of raw notes simply for being raw. A mode that fires because the work is unfinished is not a finding.

**Watch which kind of question you are answering.** Modes marked `(search)` send you back into the material and produce things you could not have written from outside it. Modes marked `(check)` are answered from memory, and are often decided by the study's genre before you open the file — an interview-only study fails 4 and 5 by construction. Run both. Only the first kind should surprise you.

When it was tested on a real government discovery synthesis, five of the sixteen produced findings a competent reviewer would plausibly have missed. Eight were decided by the study's genre. That ratio is the honest expectation.

---

## Two rules above all sixteen

They matter more when a model is doing the reading.

**Every quote must be locatable in the source**, verbatim, with its participant ID. Fabricated and composite quotes are the signature failure of machine-assisted qualitative analysis, and a report does not recover from one.

**Consent scope governs everything.** People agreed to a study, not to a repository, a publication, or a model.

## Where the names come from

Several of these have established names in survey methodology and psychology, and the file gives both. The coined name is the mnemonic; the established one is there so a methods-literate reader knows this isn't invented vocabulary. The Survivor Sample is **coverage error**. The Articulate Majority is **self-selection** at the point of consent. The Wrong Unit rests on the **fundamental attribution error**.

The contribution is the application, not the labels.
